In 2017, hospitals had to turn patients away. Everyone had a firewall.
A program called WannaCry locked the files on tens of thousands of computers in a single day, including hospital computers in Britain. Operations were cancelled. Ambulances were sent elsewhere. A repair for the exact hole it used had been released by Microsoft two months earlier, and most of the machines that were hit had never installed it.
What everyone was assuming
That danger comes from outside, so a strong wall at the edge of the building keeps it out. A firewall is a guard on the door. It checks who comes in.
Where the assumption broke
The guard on the door does not watch what happens between rooms. Once WannaCry was inside one machine, it spread computer to computer without ever going back out through the door. The wall was never wrong. It was answering a different question from the one that mattered.
This is why a whole industry exists to watch what is happening inside, and why the first thing those companies had to learn was what normal looks like.
